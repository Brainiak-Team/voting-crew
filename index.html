    Where the candidates stand, issue by issue.

    Issues below are taken from each race's Republican candidate's own campaign site. Each candidate's position — including the Democratic candidate's — is drawn from that candidate's own campaign site; a dash means the site did not address it.

    Governor: Vivek Ramaswamy (R) vs. Amy Acton (D)

    Issue comparison, Governor race
    IssueVivek Ramaswamy (R)Amy Acton (D)
    Taxes Wants to cut property taxes right away and phase out Ohio's income tax, starting with eliminating the capital-gains tax. Says her top priority is a tax cut for working families: an earned-income credit plus new child tax credits.
    Energy costs & business climate Would expand natural gas and new energy technology, streamline permitting, and cut regulations to lower electric bills and draw industry. Would lower bills by adding low-cost, clean power and naming utility regulators who favor ratepayers over utility companies.
    Education Would raise academic standards, expand parental choice in schooling, pay top teachers more, and grow career-technical education. Would restore public school funding to the state's constitutional level and keep public funding for public schools only.
    Crime & public safety Would toughen bail and sentencing for repeat violent offenders and back police to act quickly against crime. Would fully fund police and first responders and invest in mental-health and addiction treatment alongside law enforcement.
    Political tone (“One Ohio”) Frames his campaign as restoring respectful political discourse and uniting Ohioans around shared, common-sense values over partisan division. Not addressed on campaign site.
    Data centers Pledges free electricity for residents near data centers, mandatory full tax payment plus homeowner rebates, and strict environmental rules. Would require data centers to cover 100% of their own utility costs, use union labor, and meet strict environmental/transparency rules.

    Sources: Ramaswamy (R) · Acton (D)

    U.S. Senate: Jon Husted (R) vs. Sherrod Brown (D)

    Issue comparison, U.S. Senate race
    IssueJon Husted (R)Sherrod Brown (D)
    Jobs & economic development Points to helping land Ohio's largest-ever economic development deal (Intel's chip plant) and creating job-training programs like TechCred. Site criticizes past corporate tax breaks and trade deals for having shipped Ohio jobs overseas, without naming a current jobs plan.
    Taxes & government efficiency Says he founded InnovateOhio to cut government waste, and backed what was at the time the state's largest income-tax cut. Not addressed on campaign site.
    Life / values Describes himself as a lifelong "champion for life," a stance he connects to his own adoption out of foster care. Not addressed on campaign site.
    Law enforcement & public safety Says he backs police and helped secure millions of dollars in funding for law-enforcement agencies. Not addressed on campaign site.

    Sources: Husted (R) · Brown (D)

    Your privacy

    Short version: the names you type stay on your device. This site has no accounts, no cookies, no ads and no tracking, and it never sends your list anywhere.

    The names on your list

    • Names, and which ones are ticked "Going", are saved only in this browser, on this device, in its built-in "local storage". They are never uploaded. There is no copy on any server, so no one elsewhere, including the people who run this site, can see them.
    • The page's security settings let it connect only to this website, and the one request it makes (for the race polling) carries no names.
    • Your list does not follow you to other devices or browsers. Anyone who uses this browser on this device can open the page and see it.
    • To erase a name, delete its text (its "Going" tick goes with it). To erase everything, clear this site's data in your browser settings. In a private or incognito window, the list is erased when the window closes. Some browsers, Safari included, may clear it on their own if you have not visited the site in about a week.
    • Browser extensions you have installed can read the pages you visit, this one included, as on any website.
    • "Print your list!" uses your own browser's print window. Nothing is sent to us.

    What this site does not do

    • No accounts, sign-ups, emails or phone numbers.
    • No cookies, analytics, advertising, tracking pixels or social media buttons.
    • No outside scripts, fonts or images. Everything comes from this one site.
    • No access to your camera, microphone or location. The site blocks itself from asking.
    • When you follow a link away from this site, your browser is told not to reveal that you came from here.

    The race polling

    The poll numbers come from VoteHub, a free public archive of political polls (Fox News's own polls, run by Beacon Research/Shaw & Co. Research). This website's own server asks VoteHub for them and reuses each answer for every visitor for about an hour. Your browser never contacts VoteHub, and VoteHub never learns who you are or that you visited.

    What the web host sees

    This site is hosted by Vercel. As with any website, loading the page means your browser connects to Vercel's servers, which receive basic technical details: your IP address (a rough indication of location), your browser and device type, the time, and the page requested. Vercel uses this to deliver the site and protect it from attacks, and keeps it under its own privacy policy. Vercel's dashboard also shows the site's operators recent request records containing these details. They never include your list, and this site adds no analytics or tracking of its own. The connection is encrypted (HTTPS).
